Put your money to work for the long term

Money that sits in a current account holds its value in the short term, but it rarely grows. Investing means buying something that can grow over the years, such as a fund that holds many companies at once.

Growth is not guaranteed and it is not quick. People who invest well usually give it several years and use money they do not need next month.

When it works, the point is simple: your money does more than stand still, and you decide how much of the plan you want to control yourself.

  • Start with an amount you are comfortable leaving alone
  • Review your plan once a year, not every day
  • Ask us for a proposal built around your situation

The risks, in plain words

Investing can lose money. Markets fall as well as rise, and the value of what you hold can go down while you hold it.

You may get back less than you put in, and past results say nothing about what happens next.

Only invest money you will not need for [DUREE]. Keep a buffer in a savings account for the unexpected.

  • Returns are not guaranteed
  • You can get back less than you invested
  • Your horizon matters more than your timing
  • Spreading your money is safer than one single bet

Frequently asked questions

Can I lose money if I invest?
Yes. Markets move both ways and the value of your investment can fall below what you paid in. You can get back less than you invested.
How much do I need to start?
You start from [MONTANT]. You do not need a large amount on day one, and many people add a fixed amount each month. [MONTANT] is the condition that applies to your plan.
How long should I stay invested?
Treat [DUREE] as the period we recommend. If you sell earlier, you take the price of that day, which may be lower than what you paid.
What does it cost?
Costs depend on the option you choose. You see the total cost for your option before you confirm anything, so nothing is added afterwards.
Do I owe tax on my investments?
Tax treatment depends on what you hold and on your personal situation, and the rules can change. Ask your tax adviser, or contact us before you invest.
