What investing actually means

When you invest, you put money into assets such as funds, shares or bonds and leave it there for a period you choose. The value moves up and down while you hold it.

You do not need a large amount to begin. The amount is your decision, and you can add to it or stop later.

In one sentence: you open an account, choose how much to invest and for how long, and you decide when to sell.

  • You choose what to invest in, or follow a ready-made mix.
  • Your money stays reachable, but selling after a fall locks in that fall.
  • Time in the market gives a dip room to recover, and also room to happen.

What to have ready before you start

Start with money you will not need in the coming years. Keep a buffer for the unexpected somewhere you can reach it without selling an investment.

Before you begin, decide three things: the amount, the term, and what you would do if the value dropped by a third.

  • An amount you can leave invested for [DUREE] without touching it.
  • A buffer outside your investment, for costs you did not plan.
  • Your ID and your account details to hand when you open the account.

The risk, before the return

Investments fall as well as rise. You can get back less than you put in, and in the worst case you lose the amount you invested.

No one can promise a return in advance. Anyone who does is not describing investing.

Before you buy, read the key information document for what you have chosen. It sets out the risks, the costs and the [TAUX] that applies.

In one sentence: you pick the amount and the term, you see the risk and the cost of your choice, and you sell when you decide.

  • The value of your investment can go down and up.
  • Past performance says nothing about what happens next.
  • Spreading your money across different assets lowers risk, but does not remove it.
  • Your horizon matters more than the exact moment you start.

Frequently asked questions

Can I lose money by investing?
Yes. Investments can fall in value as well as rise, and you can get back less than you invested. No return is guaranteed and no one can rule a loss out.
How much do I need to start?
You start with [MONTANT]. It does not have to be a large sum; the amount is your choice and you can add to it later.
How long should I stay invested?
That depends on what you are investing for. Money you will need within [DUREE] is usually better kept somewhere you can reach it without selling during a fall.
What happens if I change my mind?
You can sell whenever you decide. The price you receive is the price at that moment, and it may be lower than what you paid in.
Do I need to know about markets?
No. You answer a few questions about your goal and how you would react if the value fell, and you see the risks in plain language before you confirm anything.
