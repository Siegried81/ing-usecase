Your money works quietly until maturity

Once the account is open, there is nothing for you to do. The rate stays where it is until the maturity date, whatever market rates do in the meantime.

At maturity you decide what happens next. You can move the money back to your current account or your savings account, or start a new term at the rate available on that day.

  • You choose the term at the start.
  • The rate does not move during the term.
  • You decide at maturity what comes next.

Term account or savings account?

A savings account keeps your money reachable and its rate can move. A term account fixes the rate for a term you agree in advance, and in return you leave the money alone until maturity.

If you will not need the money for a while, the fixed rate is the point. If you may need it sooner, a savings account is the better home for it. Both are ING accounts, and you can hold both at the same time.

  • Term account: rate fixed at [TAUX] for [DUREE].
  • Savings account: your money stays reachable at any time.
  • Savings account: the rate can change while your money is there.

Frequently asked questions

Is the rate really fixed for the whole term?
Yes. The rate you agree when you open the account stays the same until the maturity date, whatever happens to market rates during that time.
Can I take money out before the maturity date?
A term account is meant to be left alone until maturity. If you think you may need the money sooner, ask us what is possible before you open the account, and compare it with a savings account.
What happens when the term ends?
You decide. You can move the money back to your current account or savings account, or start a new term at the rate available on that day.
